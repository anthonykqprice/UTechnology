How the simulator works
  • Goals are expanded through the recipe file down to supplied items (ingots, rubber, circuits). Each recipe runs a whole number of times; extra outputs become byproducts.
  • A machine can run a recipe when its tier covers the recipe's EU/t. Each tier above that is one overclock: standard halves the time at 4× EU/t, perfect quarters it. Most stock intermediates run at 7 EU/t (ULV), so they overclock once even in LV machines.
  • Multiblocks: parallels equal counted segments (built segments up to the frame's cap), doubled by a batch bus. The frame caps the machine's voltage. Module effects apply before overclocking. A specialization lock halves duration but only for the chosen recipe.
  • Work is spread across machines as if every step runs at once in a steady pipeline. Large chunks go first, each to whichever machine would finish it soonest.
  • The finish time is the slowest of three limits: machines (the busiest group), power (total energy divided by your supply), and the chain (one unit passing through the longest sequence of steps).
  • Energy hatches are assumed to supply enough amps for all parallels. The peak draw shows what that would take.
  • Build cost counts 8 casings per segment, 13 more for the front and end aisles, and 4 frame boxes, then expands controllers, cores and casings through the same recipes.
